# 2026-08-25 19h57 · The licence rule becomes a check

Took **item 7** from `2026-08-25_19h22.what-is-not-built-yet.md`: *a licence audit of the
dependency tree*. It was owed rather than blocking, and it was done first for a reason of timing —
`app/` is next, and egui and winit will roughly triple the tree. Auditing forty crates is cheap;
auditing two hundred after building on them is not, and if something had not fitted, the moment to
find out was before.

The audit passes. The interesting half is what it turned into.

## Where this repo actually was

Six crates, 85 third-party crates across two targets, and a rule in `AGENTS.md` that had been
there since the licence was settled — *"do not add a dependency whose licence is incompatible with
GPL-3.0-or-later"* — that nothing had ever checked. The register named it as owed and the
`Verified, and not` said plainly: *"The dependency tree has never been licence-audited."*

## Walkthrough · as built

### The finding, first, because it is short

**Every one of the 85 crates is permissive and compatible with GPL-3.0-or-later.** No copyleft, no
proprietary, no crate with an empty `license` field. Nothing had to be dropped and nothing had to
be argued for.

Two things in the result are worth carrying rather than filing:

- **Two crates are Apache-2.0-only** — `codespan-reporting` and `spirv`, both reached through
  `naga`, which is wgpu's shader compiler. Apache-2.0 is compatible with GPL-**3** and *not* with
  GPL-2. So the licence chosen for the kernel's sake, through LGPL-2.1 §3, is also the licence the
  renderer's dependencies require. **There is no version of this project that is GPL-2**, and that
  is now a fact about the tree rather than a preference.
- **`cargo metadata` cannot see the things most likely to matter.** OCCT itself is LGPL-2.1-only.
  The header `make occt-headers` fetches is OCCT's. Playwright is Apache-2.0 and is a test driver.
  Chromium and Mesa are installed to run tests. An audit that reports only what cargo knows is an
  audit that omits the only copyleft dependency in the project.

### It is a check, not a report

`tools/licences.py`, and `make licences` is part of `make test`. The rule had held for three
sessions on the strength of nobody having broken it, which is the same footing `make wasm` was on
before it started grepping for `glow`.

Four decisions in it, each because the obvious version would have been wrong:

- **Both targets are read.** The dependency sets differ — `slotmap` and `slab` are wasm-only,
  `libloading` and `wayland-sys` are host-only — and a crate that appears only on wasm is exactly
  the one nobody looks at.
- **The `license` field is an SPDX *expression*, not a string.** `MIT OR Apache-2.0` is a choice,
  so a crate is fine if *some* satisfiable choice lands inside the allowlist; `MIT AND GPL-2.0-only`
  is not, because an `AND` cannot be escaped by choosing. Matching strings would have rejected
  half of crates.io or accepted the wrong half. The parser handles parentheses, `WITH` exceptions,
  and the pre-SPDX `MIT/Apache-2.0` separator that still appears in two crates.
- **The allowlist is a list of licences someone read**, not a list that looked familiar, and an
  unknown licence fails. `MPL-2.0` is deliberately *not* on it: it is usually GPL-compatible
  through its §3.3, but the "Incompatible With Secondary Licenses" variant is not and the metadata
  field cannot tell them apart. That one needs a human, and failing is how it gets one.
- **What cargo cannot see is printed in the report**, every run, rather than left out because it
  could not be automated.

### The negative controls, which are the point

Fourteen of them, in the file, run before the verdict. A checker that cannot fail is a checker
that says yes, and this project has already been bitten by exactly that: the SIMD probe in
`web/loader.js` validated nothing and would have reported "no SIMD128" on every browser forever.

Verified by breaking it on purpose. With `satisfiable` forced to return `True`, five of the
fourteen controls fail — `GPL-2.0-only`, `MIT AND GPL-2.0-only`, `AGPL-3.0-or-later`, `MPL-2.0`
and an unlisted licence — and the tool exits 2 with *"the checker is broken; its verdict below
means nothing until this passes"* before it says anything about the real tree.

## Bugs found by building, not by reading

None. The tree was clean, the parser was right on the first real run, and the only thing that
"failed" was the deliberately broken copy.

That is worth one line rather than none: three of the last four sessions found a defect in
whatever measured the thing being measured, and this one did not. The reason is probably that the
controls were written before the verdict was trusted rather than after it looked good.

## Verified, and not

**Verified.** `make test` is 32 tests plus the licence check, and the check reports 85 crates
across two targets with no failures. Its fourteen negative controls pass, and were confirmed to
fail when the allowlist is sabotaged. `make wasm`, `make test-occt` and `make web-test` are
unchanged and still green.

**Not verified, and the risk taken with each.**

- **This is not legal advice and no lawyer has seen it.** Same caveat as the licence decision
  itself, and it applies more here: the tool encodes one reading of compatibility, and the reading
  is the FSF's.
- **The tool trusts the `license` field.** It does not read a single LICENSE file. A crate that
  declares MIT and ships something else would pass, and nothing here would notice — which is the
  standard limitation of every tool of this kind, `cargo-deny` included.
- **`license_file`-only crates would be reported as having no licence and fail**, which is the
  right default, but none exist in this tree so that path has never run against a real crate.
- **Version pinning is not licence pinning.** A crate can change its licence between versions.
  The check runs on whatever `Cargo.lock` resolves to today; it will catch a change the next time
  it runs, which is every `make test`, and that is the whole of the defence.
- **The non-cargo list is maintained by hand.** Nothing enforces that it stays complete. Adding a
  system library without adding it there produces a report that is quietly wrong.
- **npm is not checked at all.** Playwright is listed because someone wrote it down. If
  `web/test/` grows a second devDependency, nothing notices.

## Loose ends, deliberately left

- **No `cargo-deny`.** It does this and more — advisories, bans, duplicate detection — and it is
  the right answer eventually. It is a build-dependency-sized commitment and a compile, and the
  thing actually needed was 200 lines with no dependencies. When the tree is large enough that
  advisories matter, revisit.
- **Licence *texts* are not collected.** GPL-3 §6 wants the corresponding source shipped, and MIT
  and Apache-2.0 want their notices preserved in a distribution. Nothing assembles a NOTICE file,
  and a `.wasm` served to a browser is distribution. That is owed before there is a user, not
  before there is a build.
- **The allowlist has no expiry.** An entry someone added once looks identical to one someone
  re-read last week.
