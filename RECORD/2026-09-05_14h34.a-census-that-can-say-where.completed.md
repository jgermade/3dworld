## Where this repo actually was

The conformance suite had two halves. The contract half asked whether a backend keeps its
bookkeeping straight; the geometry half, three hours old, asked **how much material there is** — the
volume a mesh encloses, by the divergence theorem, against arithmetic.

That second half exists because a backend returning *the bounding box of its operands* had passed
the whole suite for nine days. Weighing the result caught that, and it was the right thing to write
first, because it is cheap and it catches the largest class of lie.

What it cannot see is anything that does not change a volume. A hole drilled eight millimetres from
where it was asked for encloses exactly the volume of a hole drilled in the right place. So does a
hole drilled in the wrong face. Both are the defect a user reports as "it put the hole in the wrong
place", and every check in this repository would have called them correct.

The register also carried a note, written three hours earlier and left unresolved:

## Walkthrough · as built

### 1 — What a suite is *entitled* to compare face by face

The obvious version of "compare the topology" is to record a face count and a per-face area for a
fixture and assert them. It is also wrong, and the reason is in `AGENTS.md`: everything in
`conformance.rs` has to be true of **any** correct kernel.

Face counts are not. `2026-09-05_13h14` changed the face count of a cylinder in this repository's
own truck backend, deliberately and correctly. OCCT and truck partition a boolean's result
differently and both are right. And face *ids* are a backend's own numbering, which the suite has
never compared across kernels and must not start.

Three properties survive that filter, and the whole of this work is those three:

- **Every face id names one connected region of the surface.** A face is one region. An id spanning
  two patches means two faces wear one name — and since `face_of_triangle` is what a selection, an
  ID-buffer pick and a per-face fillet are all stored against, a click on one of them lands on both.
- **The material in each plane of the boundary, and where its centre of area is.** Not per face:
  which planar faces a kernel splits a coplanar region into is its own business — a union may leave
  an L-shaped side as one face or as two, and both are correct — so faces are summed into the plane
  they lie in *first*. The area and the area-weighted centroid of a plane's material are then
  arithmetic, and no partition changes them.
- **The total area lying in no named plane.** A cylindrical wall may be one face or four; its area
  is 2·π·r·h either way.

The centroid is the half that a volume cannot reach, and it is why this was worth its length.

### 2 — The census

`census()` reduces a `Mesh` to one `Face` per id: area, area-weighted normal, centre of area,
flatness, and a count of connected regions. `check_census()` then holds that against a fixture of
`GoldenPlane`s and one number for everything else.

Two decisions in it are not obvious and were both arrived at by getting them wrong first.

**Planarity is a distance, not an angle between normals.** The natural test — every triangle normal
within some angle of the face's mean — fails on the one fixture that matters, because a constrained
Delaunay triangulation leaves slivers along a trimming curve, and a sliver's normal is numerical
noise while its distance from the plane is not. So a face is planar when its furthest *vertex* lies
within `flat` of the plane through its centroid. Measured on the drilled plate, the planar faces
come out at 5·10⁻¹⁵ and the curved ones at 1.96, against a threshold of 4·10⁻³ — twelve orders of
magnitude of margin below and five hundred times above.

**Connectivity is welded by position, with a known bias.** A tessellator may emit a shared corner
once or once per face, so "these two triangles touch" cannot be read off the index buffer. The weld
is a sort and a split on gaps, once per axis. It chains — three points at 0, `tol` and 2·`tol` come
out as one cluster — so it can **over**-merge and cannot under-merge. That bias is chosen, not
tolerated: over-merging makes a face that really is in two pieces look connected, which is a missed
defect, and under-merging would make a connected face look broken, which is a conformance suite
failing a correct kernel and by far the more expensive mistake.

### 3 — The two fixtures

**A box**, 2×4×6: six planes, areas 24, 12 and 8, each centred on its own plane, and *zero* area
anywhere else — asserted rather than skipped, because a backend whose face ids do not partition the
boundary leaves area over and there is nowhere for it to go.

**A drilled plate**, the same 40×40×10 plate and 6 mm drill that `make freecad-check` weighs against
16000 − π·6²·10 and that `w3d-kernel-truck`'s tessellation fingerprints are recorded on. The hole is
at x = 8, and **the offset is the entire point**. Centred, the plate's top face has its centre of
area at the origin whether the hole is there or not, and the assertion would be about nothing. At
x = 8 the material left in that plane is lopsided and its centre of area moves to

    -8 · π·6² / (1600 − π·6²)  =  −0.608499

which no volume, no bounding box and no face count can produce.

Like every fixture in this file that is not one of the three mandatory boxes, it is declinable: a
backend that will not drill says so and is held to nothing further. It may not answer wrongly.

### 4 — The controls, which are the part that makes it mean anything

`check_census` passes against both backends in the tree. That is equally consistent with it
asserting nothing, and `make licences` already sets the precedent — it runs fourteen negative
controls first, because a checker that cannot fail is a checker that says yes.

So the census is also run against a mesh built by hand in `kernel/`, with no kernel anywhere: a
40×40×10 plate with a **square** hole through it, whose every number is an integer, and which
exercises every path the real fixture does. Then it is broken in the three ways the census claims to
catch, and each break has to produce a failure — checked by the *reason*, not just by failing:

| Control | Break | Must fail with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `the_fixture_describes_the_plate_it_was_written_for` | none | — |
| `a_hole_in_the_wrong_place_keeps_every_area_and_is_caught_anyway` | hole moved 2 mm | `centre of area` |
| `one_id_over_two_separate_patches_is_caught` | opposite sides given one id | `disconnected regions` |
| `a_wall_that_is_not_there_is_caught` | the hole's walls deleted | `lies in no plane` |

The second is the one worth reading. It moves the hole and changes nothing else: the volume is
identical, the bounding box is identical, the face count is identical, and **every area in the
census is identical** — which the test asserts on its own account rather than claiming. Only the
centre of area moves. That is the defect this whole file was written for, and it is invisible to
every other check in this repository.

### 5 — Connectivity is contract, not geometry

The one-region rule went into the **contract** half, run against every backend including
`FakeKernel`. It is true of a backend whose faces are the six sides of a bounding box just as much
as of a real one, and a fake that got it wrong would be wrong about the thing picking depends on.

## Bugs found by building, not by reading

- **`cargo check` was reported as passing when it had refused to build.** The baseline run of
  `cargo check --all-targets` in this container printed `rustc 1.94.1 is not supported by the
  following packages` — `egui 0.36.1` and six siblings require 1.95 — and the run was recorded as
  exit 0. It was not cargo: the command was piped into `tail`, and a shell pipeline exits with the
  status of its *last* stage. Cargo exits 101, confirmed against a scratch crate with an
  unsatisfiable `rust-version`. No defect in the repository, and a real one in the method: **every
  `make`/`cargo` invocation in a session like this must have its output read, not its exit status
  believed**, because the exit status shown is the pager's. It is the same failure mode as
  `SKIPPED:` printing while `cargo test` says `ok`, one level up.
- **The container ships a toolchain below the workspace's floor.** `rustup update` moved it from
  1.94.1 to 1.98.1, which is what the register records the tree as clean on. Nothing in the repo
  states a floor: there is no `rust-toolchain.toml`, and the 1.95 requirement arrives transitively
  through egui. A fresh environment can therefore fail `make check` on a workspace member for a
  reason that has nothing to do with the change under test.
- **A `Vec3` normal is not always defined, and the census must say so rather than guess.** A face
  covering a whole cylinder has triangle normals that cancel, so the area-weighted mean is near
  zero and `normalize` correctly returns `None`. Treated as "no plane describes this face" —
  which is right, and is why the OCCT case of a single 360° lateral face is handled without ever
  having been run here.

Every tolerance in `slack_for` is that measurement with an order of magnitude on it. |

**Not verified, and the risk taken with each.**

- **`OcctKernel` has never run this.** OCCT is not installed in the environment this was written in,
  so the only thing holding the census against the other backend that does geometry is CI. The
  tolerances were chosen with that in mind — 17× truck's measured area margin, 20× its centroid
  margin — but "chosen generously" is not "measured", and the specific way it could fail is a
  tessellation coarse enough on a 6 mm hole that the inscribed polygon's shortfall exceeds 2·10⁻³.
- **Two fixtures is two.** A box and a drilled plate. Both are axis-aligned, both are convex or
  nearly so, neither is degenerate, and nothing here is a union, a fillet, a sweep or a loft. The
  suite gained the *ability* to compare a boundary; it has barely used it.
- **The centroid catches a hole 0.3 mm out of place and not one 0.1 mm out.** `centroid` is
  `size · 5·10⁻⁴`, which is 0.02 mm on this plate; a hole displaced by δ moves the plane's centre of
  area by δ · 0.0761. Tighter is available and unverified against OCCT, so it was not taken.
- **Over-merging in the weld is unmeasured.** The bias is argued for and its direction is certain;
  what has not been measured is how far a real mesh is from the point where it starts hiding a
  genuine disconnection. On the fixtures here, vertex spacing on the hole wall is ~0.28 mm against a
  weld of 4·10⁻⁴ mm, which is three orders of magnitude, but that is one mesh at one quality.
- **The census is O(faces × triangles).** Every face rescans the triangle list. It is invisible at
  6294 triangles and it is the wrong shape for an import-sized mesh, and nothing measured where
  that starts to matter.
- **Nothing asserts a plane the fixture does *not* name is absent** beyond its area landing in the
  `curved` total. A backend that invented a spurious planar face in an unnamed plane would be
  caught only by that number moving.
- **The adjudication in §6 was done by reading code, once, by one reader.** Six items were removed
  from the register on that basis. If one of them was removed wrongly, the register is now the only
  place it was written down and it is gone from there.

## Loose ends, deliberately left

- **`make test` got slower again**, though not much: the two new fixtures add a drilled plate at
  display quality to every backend that does geometry, which is ~7 s of the truck conformance run's
  17.
- **`slack_for` takes a size and nothing else.** It could take the `Quality` and derive the curved
  allowance from the sag, which would replace a measured constant with an argument. Nothing needed
  it, and the constant is recorded above with the measurement behind it.
- **The synthetic plate in the controls is square-holed.** It exercises every code path — including
  faces built of four rectangles connected only through each other — but its "curved" area is four
  flat walls in unnamed planes rather than anything curved. The name `curved` is therefore slightly
  wrong for what the parameter means, which is "area in no plane the fixture names".
- **`Topology`'s counts are still unused by any of this.** The census reads the mesh, not the
  trait's face count, precisely because the counts are not kernel-independent. `Topology` remains
  what it was: counts only, with one reader.

## Walkthrough · as completed

The suite can say **where**. Until today every check in this repository weighed a result — a
volume, a bounding box, a face count, a triangle count — and a feature in the wrong place weighs
exactly what a feature in the right place weighs.

What landed: a face census that reduces a tessellation to what is true of the faces rather than of
the mesh; a comparison at the level of *planes* rather than faces, because which faces a kernel
splits a coplanar region into is its own business and the area and centre of area of a plane's
material are not; a connectivity rule in the contract half, since a face id spanning two patches is
a click that selects the wrong thing; two golden fixtures, a box and the drilled plate the rest of
the repository already weighs three other ways; and four negative controls run with no kernel at
all, of which one moves a hole two millimetres, changes no area anywhere, and has to be caught by
the centroid alone.

And the register was adjudicated after three hours of standing self-contradictory: six items closed,
three rewritten as the half of themselves that is genuinely left — the seam not carrying an assembly
hierarchy that both ends already have, a NOTICE that is generated and never shipped, and degenerate
fixtures that exist only for the backend the browser does not use.

What did not land, and is the register's: OCCT has never run any of this, two fixtures is two, and
nothing here is degenerate or off-axis. Nothing is pending in this file.
