# 2026-09-14 00h21 · A profile that reaches the kernel

Opened by a fixture, not by the register. Writing the self-intersecting-operand check owed by item 8
— see
[`2026-09-13_23h20.where-a-boolean-is-actually-hard.completed.md`](./2026-09-13_23h20.where-a-boolean-is-actually-hard.completed.md)
— produced a number that could not be explained: a bowtie 2 x 2 extruded 1 came back enclosing
**400**, a hundred times the profile's own bounding prism. The bowtie was not the bug.

## Where this repo actually was

`extrude`, `revolve`, `sweep` and `loft` had been on the seam since 2026-08-27, with records saying
each was "fully implemented across `GeometryKernel`, `Document`, all geometry backends, and the
Ribbon UI". The app had a sketcher, an *Extrude* button, and a Ribbon group for all four.

What every backend actually did with a `Profile` was reduce it to `(kind, p1, p2)` — two numbers —
and pick a primitive:

- **A `Polygon` was thrown away entirely.** `TruckKernel::extrude` and `OcctKernel::extrude` both
  answered *any* polygon with `create_box(20, 20, distance)`; `revolve`, `sweep` and `loft` with
  10 x 10. The app's sketcher produces nothing but polygons, so **the sketcher had never extruded
  what a user drew, on any backend, since it was built**.
- **`w3d_occt_sweep` ignored the path** and returned a box 30 long. **`w3d_occt_loft` ignored the
  planes** and returned a box 20 tall. A cone between two discs came out a cylinder.
- **`revolve` ignored the axis**: a rectangle became a cylinder of the rectangle's own width, placed
  at the axis origin, whatever axis was named.
- **`TruckKernel::fillet` and `::chamfer` returned `self.copy(body)`** — a new handle, the same
  solid. A user pressed Fillet on the backend the browser runs and nothing happened.
- **`TruckKernel::shell` returned a smaller box.** Not hollow, not the same shape: a hollowed sphere
  came back a cube.

Every one of these passed the suite, and the reason is the finding: the checks asked whether a body
came back with non-empty bounds and a mesh. A copy has bounds. A slab has a mesh.

## Walkthrough · as built

**The suite weighs an operation against arithmetic.** Five checks in the geometry half, each
declinable and none skippable:

- an extrusion is the profile it was given — a 6 x 4 by 3 is 72, a disc of 5 by 4 is 314.16, and an
  **L of area 12** by 3 is 36. The L is the one that matters: 12 is neither its bounding box's 16 nor
  anything a rectangle or a circle can stand in for. It also asserts the *placement*: `+Z by
  distance` means standing on the plane, from 0 to `distance`;
- a revolution is the profile turned about the axis it was given — a disc of 2 about a line 5 away is
  a torus of 40 pi², a 4 x 6 rectangle about a line 5 away is a tube of 240 pi, **and the same
  rectangle written as a polygon must give the same answer**, which is what catches a backend that
  reads a polygon and a rectangle differently;
- a sweep is the profile carried along the path — pi * 4 * 10 for a disc of 2 taken 10;
- a loft is a **cone**, not a cylinder: two discs of 10 and 5 twenty apart enclose 3665.19, where a
  cylinder of either radius would be 6283 or 1571;
- a fillet takes material off and a chamfer of the same setback takes more. The absolute numbers
  depend on how a kernel blends a three-edge corner; the *order* does not, and neither does "not
  zero".

**`Profile::validate`, once, in the seam.** Three things every operation needs of a profile and none
of them about the backend: a positive size, three corners, and an outline that does not cross itself.
The last is why it is shared: a bowtie has no inside — its two halves wind opposite ways and its
signed area is exactly zero — so what a kernel builds from one is whatever its sweeper happens to do.
Both backends call it first, so both refuse the same sketches with the same sentence. It has its own
negative controls, including a crossing that does *not* cancel and therefore has a respectable area.

**`TruckKernel` builds real geometry.** One `profile_wire`/`profile_face_on` pair — a polygon from its
own vertices, a disc from `rsweep`, a rectangle from four lines, any of them on any `SketchPlane` —
and then `tsweep` for an extrude, `rsweep` for a revolve, `tsweep` along the path for a sweep, and
`try_wire_homotopy` plus two caps for a loft. Measured: an L extrudes to exactly 36 with bounds
`[0,4] x [0,4] x [0,3]`; a loft from 10 to 5 over 20 encloses 3662.5 against 3665.19; a swept disc
125.30 against 125.66.

**`TruckKernel` refuses what it has not got**: `fillet`, `chamfer` and `shell` (no offset surface, no
rolling ball), a `sweep` along a bent path, a `loft` of more than two sections. `Unsupported`, with
a sentence naming what is missing and pointing at the OpenCASCADE build.

**The OpenCASCADE shim takes an outline.** A new `W3dOcctProfile` carries the kind, the two numbers,
the polygon's vertices *and the plane the profile sits on* — the plane is in the profile because a
loft needs one per section. One C++ `profile_wire`/`profile_face` pair behind it, and then
`BRepPrimAPI_MakePrism` for an extrude, `BRepPrimAPI_MakeRevol` for a revolve, a prism for a straight
sweep and `BRepOffsetAPI_MakePipe` over a polyline spine for a bent one, and
`BRepOffsetAPI_ThruSections` for a loft. `w3d_occt_extrude` did not exist before; extrusion was done
on the Rust side by calling `create_box`.

**The contract says all of this now.** `Unsupported` is documented as the conforming answer from
every operation a backend has not got, and substituting is documented as not conforming — with the
reason, which is that a suite can weigh an answer against arithmetic and cannot weigh one against an
intention. `AGENTS.md` carries the same rule, and the trait states the extrude placement, the sweep's
profile position, and that **a revolve's axis must stand clear of the profile**.

**The app carries the reason up.** `Editor::fillet` and `::chamfer` reported "fillet failed on
selected objects" and threw the kernel's message away; they now show it, because "this backend has no
edge blending" sends a user to another build and the old line sent them looking for a fault in their
model.

## Bugs found by building, not by reading

- **The placeholders themselves**, above. Found by one unexplainable number.
- **A revolved solid comes out inside out — sometimes.** `rsweep`'s sense depends on which side of
  the axis the profile sits: the same face turned about a line 5 to its left and 5 to its right comes
  out facing outwards once and inwards once. Measured at -393.2 where the answer is 394.8. So
  neither always inverting nor never inverting is right, and `faced_outwards` weighs a coarse
  triangulation and flips what came out negative. The first two attempts at this were wrong for
  instructive reasons: a blanket `.not()` (which fixed the rectangle and broke the disc), and
  measuring the *wire's* winding (which cannot work — a full-turn `rsweep` gives a circle with too
  few vertices to have a winding).
- **A profile turned about an axis through itself is not a solid.** A profile is centred on the
  origin, so the suite's own revolve fixtures — both the old contract one and my first draft of the
  new one — asked for exactly that: each half of the profile sweeps over the other and the result
  covers itself twice. OpenCASCADE refuses it outright, correctly, and **the only reason those checks
  ever passed is that the backend was substituting a cylinder and never revolving anything**. Both
  fixtures now stand the axis clear, and `kernel-occt/tests/degenerate.rs` asserts the refusal as
  well, since that file exists for exactly this kind of input.
- **The fake kernel was centring its extrusions too**, and inventing 10 x 10 for a polygon. It does
  not do geometry and is excused from the geometry half, but the extent and the placement are
  contract, so it now boxes the polygon's own vertices and stands the result on the plane.
- **A test of mine, wrong twice over**: 45 where the answer is 50 for the reach of a half turn
  (the radius, not the span).

## Verified, and not

**Verified.** `make test`, `make test-occt`, `make step-check`, `make app-test`, `make app-test-step`,
and `cargo check -p w3d-web --target wasm32-unknown-unknown`. Both real backends now pass all five
arithmetic checks, or decline with `Unsupported`:

| | extrude | revolve | sweep | loft | fillet / chamfer | shell |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| OpenCASCADE | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ straight and bent | ✅ any sections | ✅ | ✅ |
| truck | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ straight only | ✅ two sections | ⬜ declined | ⬜ declined |

An end-to-end test in the app drives the sketcher — enter sketch mode, four points, Finish — and
requires the solid to span the 6 x 3 that was drawn, standing on the plane, 20 deep.

**Not verified, and the risk taken.**

- **A polygon with a hole is not expressible**, so nothing is known about one. `Profile::Polygon` is
  a single outline; an inner loop would be a second wire and a change to the seam.
- **The polygon path is exercised on convex shapes and one L.** No fixture has a hundred-vertex
  sketch, a nearly-collinear corner, or a vertex repeated.
- **`Profile::validate` is O(n²)** and runs on every operation. At sketch sizes that is nothing; at
  ten thousand points it would be seconds, and nothing measures it.
- **A revolved `truck` solid is marked `singular`** — it will not go into a boolean, because a full
  turn leaves a seam that `truck-shapeops` cannot split. That is a decline, not a wrong answer, and
  it means *revolve then cut* is an OpenCASCADE-only workflow.
- **The tolerance of the arithmetic**: curved answers are held to 2% and the loft to 3%, because a
  display-quality tessellation under-reports a curved wall. A backend could be 1% wrong and pass.
- **`faced_outwards` costs a coarse triangulation per revolve**, unmeasured.
- **Nothing asserts what an extrusion's *faces* are.** The golden census of 2026-09-05 could weigh a
  prism's boundary plane by plane, and these checks weigh only volume and extent.

## Loose ends, deliberately left

- **Fillet, chamfer and shell are gone from the browser and from the default desktop build**, which
  is `--features truck`. They were never there — they returned the body unchanged — but the buttons
  are still in the Ribbon and now report a refusal instead of pretending. Disabling a button a
  backend cannot serve needs a capability query on the seam, which is a wider change than this.
- **A bent sweep and a three-section loft** are OpenCASCADE-only for the same reason.
- **`truck`'s own boolean still declines coplanar overlap**, so a chamfer built as "a plane and a
  boolean" would not work there either — which is why `chamfer` refuses rather than being faked a
  second way.
- **`Profile` has no arcs.** A sketch of straight lines and whole circles is what the seam can say.

## Walkthrough · as completed

What was meant to be one fixture for a self-intersecting profile turned out to be the discovery that
**four of the seam's operations had never been implemented on either real backend** — and that the
suite's shape made it invisible, because every check asked whether an answer arrived rather than what
it weighed.

Landed: arithmetic checks for all six operations, one shared `Profile::validate`, real wire-based
geometry in both backends, honest refusals where a backend has nothing, the contract and `AGENTS.md`
amended to say that substituting is not conforming, and the app showing the kernel's reason. The
sketcher now extrudes the shape that was drawn — which it had never done.

Everything it leaves owed is in the live register,
[`2026-08-25_21h59.what-is-not-built-yet.md`](./2026-08-25_21h59.what-is-not-built-yet.md): the
operations `truck` declines and what they cost a browser user, the Ribbon buttons with no capability
query behind them, a profile with a hole, and arcs. Nothing here was abandoned.
