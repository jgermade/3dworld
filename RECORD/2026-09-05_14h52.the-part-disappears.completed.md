It was meant to be a suite-widening session. It found a bug in `TruckKernel` on the first probe, and
that is most of what this file is about.

## Where this repo actually was

Three hours earlier the conformance suite gained a census that compares a *boundary* — area per
plane, centre of area, total area in no named plane, and the rule that a face id names one connected
region. Two fixtures used it: a box and a drilled plate, both axis-aligned, neither degenerate.

Degenerate coverage existed in exactly one place: `kernel-occt/tests/degenerate.rs`, eight fixtures,
run under `make test-occt` and nowhere else. `TruckKernel` — the backend that is actually in the
browser, whose boolean is narrow and which panics rather than failing on geometry it cannot
handle — had none, and neither did anything in `make test`.

## Walkthrough · as built

### 1 — Measure first

`AGENTS.md` says a failure from a second backend "is a question about the suite first", so nothing
was asserted before something was observed. A scratch probe ran fifteen boolean pairs through
`TruckKernel` — coincident faces, corner contact, a tool wholly inside, wholly disjoint operands,
a cylinder tangent to a box face — and printed what came back, rather than testing a guess.

Most of it was the expected picture of a narrow boolean: declines, in the honest form the backend
already had. Four rows were not.

### 2 — The bug: a difference by a tool that misses deletes the part

```
disjoint      Difference   OK  tessellate failed: empty mesh
corner touch  Difference   OK  tessellate failed: empty mesh
```

A 2 mm cube minus an identical cube **50 mm away** returned `Ok`, carrying a body with:

```
bounds     = Err(Failed("empty bounds"))
topology   = Ok(Topology { solids: 1, faces: 0, edges: 0, vertices: 4 })
tessellate = Err(Failed("empty mesh"))
```

The correct answer is the cube. The tool removes nothing because it touches nothing.

The cause is in the shape of the delegation, not in a number. `TruckKernel::boolean` implements
`Difference` as `and(A, not(B))` — there is no third operation in `truck-shapeops` and there does not
need to be — and `truck_shapeops::and` answers with an **empty solid** whenever the two boundaries
never meet. For an intersection that is right. For a difference, A lies wholly inside the inverted B
and the answer is the whole of A, so it is exactly wrong. One library call is correct and incorrect
depending on which of two callers reached it, and both callers were the same line.

This is a user-facing defect of the worst kind available to a modeller: **drag a drill off the edge
of the part, and the part disappears.** No error, no warning; `Ok`, and an empty document node.

### 3 — The fix: decide the separable case, do not delegate it

A pre-flight in `TruckKernel::boolean`, before anything reaches `truck-shapeops`. If the operands'
**bounding boxes** do not overlap in a set of positive volume, they provably share no interior point,
and then:

- `Difference` → a copy of the minuend. Exact, and free.
- `Intersection` → empty, and there is no empty `Body` in this contract, so it is **refused** rather
  than answered with something a caller could mistake for geometry. `conformance` has accepted
  "empty or refused" since the self-difference check was written, and this is the honest half of it.
- `Union` → **not** shortcut. Two solids meeting on a face are a real merge with a real seam to
  build, and `truck_shapeops::or` already answers the wholly-separate case correctly.

Boxes that overlap in a set of *zero* volume — touching on a face, an edge or a corner — count as
separable, which is right: a solid is a closed regular set, and removing a measure-zero slice of its
boundary leaves it alone. Overlapping boxes prove nothing and are left alone, so this is a shortcut
on the certain half and never a guess on the other.

What it did, measured on the same probe:

| Case | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `A − B`, 50 mm apart | `Ok`, empty body | `Ok`, volume 8 |
| `A − B`, touching on a face | `Ok`, empty body | `Ok`, volume 8 |
| `A − B`, touching at a corner | `Ok`, empty body | `Ok`, volume 8 |
| `A − B`, cylinder tangent to a box face | declined | `Ok`, volume 1000 |
| `A ∩ B`, all four of the above | `Ok`, empty body / declined | refused, by name |

Four wrong answers became right ones, and four bodies that were `Ok` and empty became refusals that
say why.

### 4 — The checks, so it cannot come back

Five new checks, taking the suite from 37 to 42.

- **"a difference by a tool that misses leaves the minuend whole"** — three separations, because
  they fail differently: wholly apart, touching on a face, touching at a single corner. The last two
  are the ones a bounding-box test alone would call an overlap.
  It is deliberately **not** tolerant of a tessellation failure, which is a departure from the
  neighbouring checks. Where a result is *meant* to be empty this suite accepts a body that will not
  mesh; here the result is the whole minuend, so a body with no geometry in it is the defect and not
  an excuse for it. That distinction is the entire reason the check catches the bug.
- **"an intersection of bodies that share no volume is empty or refused"** — the mirror, and worth
  having precisely because the same delegation that made the difference wrong makes this one right.
  A backend can pass this and fail that.
- **"a union of two separate bodies is both of them"** — 16, not 8 and not a bounding box.
- **"a wall thin enough to be a sheet is still a solid"** — 100 × 100 × 0.01, ten thousand to one,
  where a tessellator working in absolute units collapses a solid and a boolean's tolerance can
  exceed the feature it is meant to resolve.
- **"the same plate, off every axis, is still the same plate"** — the drilled plate rotated 30° about
  Z and 20° about X, so that not one face is axis-aligned and not one coordinate is a round number.

### 5 — Why the off-axis fixture is two checks in one

It asks two unrelated questions and it is worth being explicit that they are unrelated.

The first is about the **census**: every number it computes — an area, a centre of area, a distance
from a plane — is supposed to be independent of the frame it is measured in, and a suite that only
ever sees axis-aligned fixtures cannot tell that from an implementation that quietly assumes one.

The second is about the **kernel**: a rotation is exact on paper and is a matrix multiplication in
`f32` by the time it reaches the mesh, so this is where a transform that is subtly not rigid shows up
as a face that has changed size.

The rotation is applied **after** the boolean, not to the operands. The other order would be a
weaker fixture: it would let a backend that only works on axis-aligned input pass by doing the cut in
the frame it likes.

`drilled_plate_golden` carries the fixture through the matrix, and the three quantities go through
differently — a normal as a direction, a centroid as a point, and the offset **recomputed** from the
placed pair rather than carried at all, because a translation changes a plane's distance from the
origin while leaving its normal alone.

### 6 — Both new fixtures were made to fail on purpose

The suite passed against both backends after the fix, which is equally consistent with the new checks
asserting nothing. So each was falsified before being believed:

- The backend fix was **temporarily reverted** and the suite re-run. It failed exactly one check —
  `a difference by a tool that misses leaves the minuend whole` — with the message naming the empty
  body. The check catches the bug it was written for.
- The drilled plate's golden centroid was **perturbed by 0.1 mm** and the suite re-run. It failed two
  checks, the axis-aligned fixture and the rotated one, the second reporting a plane whose normal is
  `(0.171, −0.296, 0.940)` — genuinely off every axis — and a centre of area 0.0990 from where the
  fixture puts it. The rotated fixture is doing real work in a rotated frame.

## Bugs found by building, not by reading

- **`truck_shapeops::and` returns an empty solid when the boundaries never meet, and a difference is
  an `and`.** The whole of §2. It had been in the tree since the boolean landed three hours earlier,
  it was not visible in any volume, bounding box, face count or browser check, and the fixture that
  found it is the second-simplest boolean anybody would try.
- **The three mandatory box fixtures cannot catch this, by construction.** They are two *overlapping*
  boxes, chosen so their union, difference and intersection are arithmetic. Every one of them takes
  the delegating path. The floor under "a backend may decline" was doing its job and the job did not
  include this.
- **An `Ok` carrying a body that cannot be tessellated is not obviously a failure**, and the suite had
  a precedent for treating it as acceptable — the self-difference check, where an empty result is the
  *right* answer. Reusing that leniency here would have written a check that the buggy backend
  passed. The two cases look identical at the call site and differ entirely in what they mean.

**Not verified, and the risk taken with each.**

- **`OcctKernel` has run none of this**, for the second session running: OCCT is not installed here.
  Five new checks now apply to it and CI is the only thing that will say. The one most likely to
  surprise is the union of two separate bodies — OCCT's `Fuse` returns a compound of two solids, and
  whether the shim's `tessellate` meshes both of them has never been asserted anywhere. If it fails,
  that is a finding and not a false alarm; a modeller must be able to union two separate bodies.
- **The fix is a bounding-box test, so it is exact only where boxes are.** Two solids whose boxes
  overlap but which share no material — a ring around a rod, an L against its own mirror — still go
  to `truck-shapeops` and still get whatever it gives. The class of silently-wrong differences is
  narrowed, not closed, and nothing here measures how much of it is left.
- **Nothing was fixed in `truck-shapeops`.** The defect is upstream and the shim now routes around
  the case it is certain about. An upstream fix would be better and is not ours to make in this tree.
- **`tool inside` is still declined, in all three operations.** A 10-cube minus a 2-cube wholly
  inside it — a solid with an internal cavity — returns `None` from `truck-shapeops` and is refused.
  That is honest and it is a real gap: a cavity is a thing modellers make, and this backend cannot.
  Not asserted either way, because refusing conforms.
- **`coincident face Union` is still declined.** Two cubes sharing exactly a face should fuse into
  one 2×2×4 box, volume 16. `truck-shapeops::or` returns `None`. Left as a decline, which conforms,
  and named in the register.
- **The off-axis fixture is one rotation.** 30° about Z composed with 20° about X. No translation
  away from the origin, no scale, no near-degenerate angle. `f32` mesh positions are absolute, so a
  fixture placed kilometres out would be a different and harder question — and it is already in the
  register under a different name.
- **The thin-wall fixture is 10⁴:1 and nothing thinner.** The ratio at which this actually breaks was
  not searched for.

## Loose ends, deliberately left

- **The probe is not in the tree.** Fifteen boolean pairs, printed rather than asserted; it is how
  the bug was found and it asserts nothing, so it was deleted rather than committed. What survives
  of it is the five checks and the table in §3.
- **The separable pre-flight computes bounds twice per boolean.** `boolean_tolerance` already asks
  for both operands' bounds a few lines later. Two calls where one would do, on a path whose other
  end is a general boolean, so it was not worth the coupling to fix.
- **The suite is 42 checks and takes ~24 s in debug against truck**, up from ~17. The rotated plate
  is a second full drilled plate at display quality and is most of the difference.
- **`drilled_plate_golden` requires `m` to be rigid** and says so in a doc comment rather than
  asserting it. A non-rigid matrix would change every area and the failure would be reported as a
  wrong area rather than as a misused helper.

## Extension · 2026-09-05 · OCCT ran it, and it passed

This file and

both closed with the same risk outstanding, in the same words: `OcctKernel` had run none of it,
because OCCT is not installable in the environment either was written in, and CI was the only thing
that would say. CI has said.

Run 64 of `ci.yml` on commit `207aeda`, the `make test-occt + step-check` job, against the
distribution's OpenCASCADE **7.6.3**:

```
Running tests/conformance.rs
test opencascade_conforms ... ok
```

`conformance.rs` calls `assert_passed`, which panics listing *every* failure rather than the first,
so an `ok` there is not a summary — it is all 42 checks. `OcctKernel::does_geometry` returns `true`,
so the geometry half ran, and that half is where all of both sessions' work lives: the golden census,
the box and drilled-plate fixtures, the plate rotated 30° about Z and 20° about X, and the four
degenerate fixtures. The rest of the job was green too — 8 degenerate, 6 document, 3 format, 7 STEP
and 2 viewport tests, and `clippy -D warnings` over the FFI crate.

**Nothing had to be adjusted for it**, which is the part worth recording. Two things were written
against one backend and guessed at for the other, and both guesses held:

- The **tolerances** in `slack_for` were measured against `TruckKernel` and given an order of
  magnitude for OCCT's sake, with the specific fear named at the time: a tessellation coarse enough
  on a 6 mm hole that the inscribed polygon's shortfall exceeds 2·10⁻³. It did not.
- The **union of two separate bodies** was flagged as the check most likely to surprise, because
  OCCT's `Fuse` returns a compound of two solids and nothing had ever asserted that the shim's
  `tessellate` meshes both. It does.

What this does *not* establish, and the closed files were right to say so: the fixtures are still
two backends agreeing on a small set of well-behaved shapes. Nothing here is degenerate on the
overlapping side of the separable line, nothing is placed far from the origin, and no third kernel
exists to disagree with the two that now concur. Agreement between two implementations is evidence
about the suite, not proof about the geometry.

The correction this earns is to one sentence in each file's `Verified, and not` — **"`OcctKernel`
has run none of this"** was true when written and is not true now. Per `AGENTS.md` the original text
stays standing; this is where the answer lives, and the live register has been updated, since it is
the one document that is maintained rather than appended.
