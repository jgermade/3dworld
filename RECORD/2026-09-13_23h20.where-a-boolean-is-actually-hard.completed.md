## Where this repo actually was

The suite had five degenerate fixtures running against every backend, all on one side of a line the
register drew itself: **separable** operands — a tool that misses, bodies that share no volume, two
cubes touching on a face or at a corner, a wall ten thousand times wider than it is thick. That half
was written first because a defect in it deletes a part, and
[`2026-09-05_14h52.the-part-disappears.completed.md`](./2026-09-05_14h52.the-part-disappears.completed.md)
found exactly such a defect. It is not the half where a B-rep kernel is hard.

The register named what was missing, and this took all five: coincident faces that *overlap*,
tangent surfaces sharing a curve, near-parallel faces at a thousandth of a degree, a
self-intersecting operand, and a body far enough from the origin for absolute `f32` mesh positions
to matter.

## Walkthrough · as built

Four fixtures in `kernel/src/conformance.rs`, in the **geometry half**, so every backend that claims
geometry runs them and the fake is excused. Each is weighed against arithmetic and each may be
declined — a decline conforms, a wrong answer does not:

- **Two 4-cubes overlapping with four pairs of coplanar faces**, in all three operations: 96, 32 and
  32. Coplanar faces with material behind both of them is the case a kernel must resolve rather than
  route around, and the union is what catches a backend that returns the intersection for the
  difference, since those two numbers are the same here.
- **A cylinder tangent to a box face along a whole line** — the box plus the cylinder for a union,
  the box untouched for a difference.
- **Two cubes abutting with one turned 0.001°**, so the faces that were coincident meet in a wedge
  3.5e-5 wide: three hundred times the document's linear tolerance, a thousand times below anything
  a user can see, and the definition of a sliver.
- **A 2 mm cube a kilometre from the origin**, where the kernel's `f64` must be exact and the mesh's
  absolute `f32` must stay within a few multiples of its own spacing.

The fifth — a self-intersecting operand — went to
[`2026-09-14_00h21.a-profile-that-reaches-the-kernel.completed.md`](./2026-09-14_00h21.a-profile-that-reaches-the-kernel.completed.md),
because writing it is what found that no backend was building profiles at all.

## Bugs found by building, not by reading

- **A fixture that could not fail.** The first draft of the far-from-origin check put the cube at
  exactly `1e6` and passed with the volume *exact*. An integer is representable in an `f32` up to
  2^24, so every corner landed on a grid point and nothing was lost. The distance is now
  `1_234_567.891`, which puts every corner between two representable values.
- **An invariant that is not one.** The first draft required two placements of a part to have the
  same bounding box. They do not: a box is not invariant under rotation. The fixture in the other
  file compares enclosed volume, which is.
- **`TruckKernel` declines the commonest boolean a user does.** Two axis-aligned boxes that overlap
  and share coplanar faces — the shape of nearly every real cut — fail in all three operations at a
  tolerance of 0.004. So does the sliver pair. This is new: the register recorded "box against box
  in all three operations" from a fixture whose boxes were in general position.
- **Tangency, which the register had recorded as a decline, is not one.** A cylinder tangent to a box
  face unions correctly on `TruckKernel`: 9569.08 against 9570.80, and the difference exactly 8000.
  The earlier measurement stands for the geometry it measured; this is a different placement.

## Verified, and not

**Verified.** `make test` (all three backends), `make test-occt`, `make step-check`, `make app-test`
and `make app-test-step`. OpenCASCADE answers all four fixtures exactly, including the 3.5e-5 sliver.
`TruckKernel` answers the tangent pair and the far-from-origin case and declines the other two,
which is conforming and is now written into the check itself rather than into a note.

**Not verified, and the risk taken.**

- **A decline is a pass, so two of these four assert nothing about `TruckKernel`.** That is the
  contract working as designed and it means the browser's backend has no *positive* coverage of
  coplanar overlap or slivers. What it has is a guarantee it will not answer wrongly.
- **The far-from-origin check stops at a kilometre**, because beyond that the limit is this crate's
  own `Mesh` rather than any backend. Measured with OpenCASCADE, whose geometry is exact in `f64`
  at every distance: at 1.2e6 the mesh is drawn 0.016 mm from where the body is; at 8.4e6, 0.2 mm;
  at 1.7e7, 0.6 mm; and at **3.4e7 — 34 km in a millimetre document — the mesh encloses zero
  volume**, because the `f32` spacing there is 4 mm and the cube is 2 mm, so both of its faces land
  on one grid line. A part that vanishes from the viewport while the kernel still holds it.
- **Nothing here tests a tolerance other than the default.** The sliver is 3.5e-5 against a linear
  tolerance of 1e-7 and a `truck` boolean tolerance of 0.004; a document at a different scale would
  put those in a different order.
- **`kernel-occt/tests/degenerate.rs` still has its eight and they still run only under
  `make test-occt`.** Four of them now have equivalents-or-better in the shared suite; they stay
  where they are because they also assert OpenCASCADE's own face counts, which is not something the
  shared suite may assert.

## Loose ends, deliberately left

- **Coplanar overlap and slivers on the browser's backend** are declined rather than answered, and
  the decline is at the tolerance `truck-shapeops` imposes (`largest_extent * 1e-3`). Whether a
  finer tolerance would let them through is unmeasured.
- **No fixture crosses the origin at a coarse tolerance**, and no fixture is both far from the origin
  *and* degenerate.
